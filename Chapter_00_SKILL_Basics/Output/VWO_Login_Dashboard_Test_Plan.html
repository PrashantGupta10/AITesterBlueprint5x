1. Objective

The objective is to verify that the VWO login dashboard provides secure, reliable, and low-friction access to the VWO platform. Testing will check that authentication and recovery flows behave as specified, validation and feedback are understandable, the interface works across supported form factors, and security, accessibility, and performance requirements are addressed.

Testing also evaluates the transition to the authenticated VWO dashboard, tracking of login outcomes, and the stated service and experience targets. This plan is based only on the supplied PRD; requirements without implementation detail or measurable acceptance values are identified for confirmation.

2. Scope

In scope

  • Login page presentation, VWO branding, announcement banner, registration link, email and password fields, and Remember Me control.
  • Email/password authentication, field validation, password feedback/requirements, failed-login handling, loading states, and successful dashboard navigation.
  • Password reset and recovery, including secure reset-token behavior and confirmation/error states.
  • Optional two-factor authentication (2FA), enterprise SSO, and social identity provider integrations where implemented and configured for the test environment.
  • Session creation, persistence, timeout, logout/expiry behavior, and preservation of recent activity/context where supported.
  • Responsive/mobile behavior, light and dark themes, keyboard and screen-reader access, clickable labels, and high-contrast/accessibility behavior.
  • Transport/session security, rate limiting, privacy/compliance handling, event analytics, support paths, availability, and performance/load characteristics.
  • Cross-browser/device compatibility, integration, regression, smoke/sanity, usability, and exploratory testing.

Out of scope / dependent on future scope confirmation

  • Biometric authentication, adaptive/risk-based authentication, and Progressive Web App behavior are listed as future enhancements and are not included in this baseline plan.
  • Testing of the broader VWO experimentation, personalization, and analytics product beyond the login handoff and explicitly identified login-related integrations.
  • Formal certification of GDPR, CCPA, WCAG, or enterprise compliance. QA can verify agreed product controls and evidence, but compliance sign-off belongs to the responsible compliance/security stakeholders.
Scope dependencies: The PRD does not identify the deployed build, test URLs, supported browser/version matrix, SSO providers/configurations, 2FA methods, password policy values, session timeout values, load profile, or defect severities. These must be confirmed before final test-case sign-off.

Test design techniques

  • Equivalence partitioning and boundary value analysis for email, password, token, and configured rate-limit/session values.
  • Decision tables for credential outcome, 2FA/SSO outcomes, Remember Me selection, and recovery states.
  • State-transition and use-case testing for login, session expiry, password reset, and identity-provider redirects.
  • Error guessing and exploratory testing for interrupted requests, repeated submissions, browser navigation, and recovery edge cases.

Acceptance targets from the PRD

  • Login page load: within 2 seconds on a standard connection; the measurement method, region, and percentile need agreement.
  • Login success rate: target of 95% or higher; define eligible attempts and exclusions before interpreting the metric.
  • Availability: stated target of 99.9%; test window, measurement source, and responsibility boundary need agreement.
  • Concurrency: support for thousands of simultaneous login attempts; exact concurrency, ramp-up, duration, and acceptable latency/error rate are TBD.
  • User satisfaction target (90%+) and support-ticket reduction target (20%) are product/business measures requiring post-release measurement, not standalone pre-release pass/fail gates.

5. Defect reporting procedure

  1. Compare the observed result with the approved requirement, design, security policy, or acceptance criterion. Record deviations, broken user journeys, accessibility barriers, and technical failures as defects.
  2. Log each defect in the project-approved tracking system with a concise title, environment/build, preconditions, numbered reproduction steps, expected and actual results, impact, reproducibility, and evidence (screenshot, sanitized logs, or trace as appropriate).
  3. Do not include passwords, session tokens, reset links, or unnecessary personal data in defect records. Sanitize evidence and follow the organization's approved handling process.
  4. Triage with QA, product, engineering, and security as appropriate. Assign severity/priority using the team's agreed definitions; security defects follow the approved security-reporting route.
  5. Retest fixes in the target build, run relevant regression coverage, and close only when the acceptance criteria pass. Reopen with updated evidence if the issue persists.
  6. Provide the agreed execution/defect status to stakeholders at the agreed cadence. The PRD does not define a tracking tool, named owners, or reporting cadence; these are TBD.
Suggested triage dimensionGuidance to confirm with project team
CriticalPotential unauthorized access, broad account/session compromise, or release-blocking outage; immediate security/engineering escalation.
HighCore login, recovery, 2FA/SSO, or dashboard handoff is unavailable or materially incorrect with no acceptable workaround.
MediumImportant secondary behavior or usability/accessibility issue with a viable workaround.

6. Test strategy

  1. Requirement analysis: Review the PRD with product, engineering, security, accessibility, and operations stakeholders. Resolve TBDs, map requirements to scenarios, and agree pass/fail criteria.
  2. Shift-left preparation: Review authentication, recovery, session, and integration designs early; prepare test data, accessibility checks, and security/performance scope before the build is ready.
  3. Build acceptance: Run smoke tests on each candidate build. If a critical primary path fails, report the build as not ready for deeper execution and provide a concise failure summary.
  4. Functional and integration testing: Execute prioritized scenarios across credential login, validation, recovery, session behavior, configured 2FA/SSO/social flows, analytics/support integrations, and dashboard handoff.
  5. UX, compatibility, and accessibility: Test responsive layout, themes, keyboard and assistive-technology use, and the agreed browser/device matrix.
  6. Security and privacy testing: Verify approved controls and safe outcomes against the agreed security checklist and test authorization. Confirm encrypted transport, safe session handling, rate limiting, and absence of sensitive information in user-facing errors or telemetry.
  7. Performance and resilience: Measure page load and login service behavior using an agreed profile; exercise concurrency, dependency delay/failure, retry behavior, and recovery. Compare results with agreed thresholds.
  8. Regression and retesting: Retest fixes and execute risk-based regression on changed and critical journeys. Repeat cycles until exit criteria are met or a documented release exception is approved.
  9. Reporting: Maintain traceability from PRD requirement to scenario, result, defect, and evidence. Share progress, blockers, and residual risks with stakeholders.

Testing types: smoke/sanity, functional, validation, integration, end-to-end, regression/retest, usability/UI, accessibility, compatibility, security, performance/load, and exploratory testing.

9. Entry and exit criteria

Requirement analysis

Entry: PRD and available designs/API or identity-integration details have been shared with QA.

Exit: Requirements are reviewed and understood; ambiguities, assumptions, acceptance thresholds, dependencies, and owners are documented.

Test design / readiness

Entry: Scope and requirement questions are sufficiently defined to create meaningful scenarios.

Exit: Prioritized scenarios, data needs, traceability, and required environment access are reviewed; blocking test-readiness gaps are resolved or explicitly accepted.

Test execution

Entry: Candidate build and test environment are available; smoke test passes; test accounts and required integrations work; approved cases and security/load authorization are in place.

Exit: Planned critical/high-priority coverage is executed or dispositioned; defects are logged; fixes are retested; no open release-blocking defect remains unless an authorized exception is documented; results and residual risks are reported.

Test closure

Entry: Test execution and defect status are available and the planned cycle is complete or formally stopped.

Exit: Test summary, coverage, open defects, known limitations, evidence, and recommendation are delivered and acknowledged by the designated approver.

10. Test execution and closure

Execute in risk order: basic login and dashboard handoff; validation and error handling; password recovery; session/Remember Me; enabled 2FA and SSO/social integrations; accessibility, responsive, and browser coverage; security/privacy checks; then performance and resilience. Run smoke coverage for each candidate build and risk-based regression after changes.

Record each result as pass, fail, blocked, or not run, with build/environment and evidence. A blocked or not-run critical scenario must be visible in the summary and cannot be counted as passed. Close the cycle only after the closure exit criteria above are satisfied or a documented exception is approved.
